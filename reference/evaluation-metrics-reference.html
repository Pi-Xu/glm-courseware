First Principle of Binary Classification

A binary model places every real \(0\) () and real \(1\) (×) along \([0.0, 1.0]\). Generally speaking, the stronger a model’s capability is, the more cleanly the two classes stay near opposite ends: true \(0\)s () gather near \(0.0\), and true \(1\)s (×) gather near \(1.0\). (Explore interactively in Lesson 0002 §1.)

1. The Master Map: Four Categories of Model Performance

A binary GLM outputs continuous risk probabilities \(s_i = \hat{\mu}_i \in (0, 1)\). Following the classic evaluation framework Steyerberg et al., 2010, "model performance" divides into four distinct categories—never use a metric from one category to answer a question in another:

Performance Category Needs Cutoff \(t\)? Plain-English Question Primary Metrics
1. Discrimination
(Ranking / Separation)		 No Does the model assign higher scores to real \(1\)s than to real \(0\)s? ROC-AUC (general pairs), PR-AUC (rare \(1\)s)
2. Calibration
(Probability Honesty)		 No When the model predicts \(0.30\), does the event actually happen ~\(30\%\) of the time? Calibration Curve (45° plot), ECE, Calibration Intercept & Slope
3. Overall Probabilistic
(Discrimination + Calibration)		 No How close is each probability \(s_i\) to true \(y_i \in \{0,1\}\)? (Requires both Cat 1 & Cat 2!) Log-Loss (\(= \frac{\text{GLM Deviance}}{2n}\)), Brier Score \(\frac{1}{n}\sum(y_i-s_i)^2\)
4. Cutoff Action / Utility
(Hard Yes/No Decisions)		 Yes Once we pick ONE cutoff \(t\) to trigger Yes/No actions, what mistakes occur? Accuracy, Recall (TPR), FPR, Precision, F1, Net Benefit

2. Diagnosis Matrix: Discrimination vs. Calibration & AUC Tiers

Crucial rule: Category 1 (Discrimination) and Category 2 (Calibration) are independent—either one can be \(100\%\) perfect while the other is broken!

Four Concrete Model Archetypes

  • High AUC + Honest Calibration: Ideal GLM—ranks cases accurately and outputs trustworthy probabilities.
  • High AUC + Bad Calibration (e.g., \(s_i\) divided by \(5\)): Ranks queues fine, but raw risk numbers lie (\(\text{Log-Loss}\) & default \(t=0.50\) break!).
  • AUC = 0.50 + Perfect Calibration (constant base-rate predictor, e.g., always predicting \(0.50\) when \(50\%\) are sick): Honest group average, zero individual separation.
  • High AUC + Overconfident \(0.01/0.99\): Looks fine on AUC & Accuracy, but one wrong \(0.99\) bet blows up Log-Loss!

AUC Discrimination Tiers (Hosmer & Lemeshow, 2013)

  • \(\text{AUC} < 0.50\): Worse than random (scores point backward).
  • \(\text{AUC} = 0.50\): Zero discrimination (coin flip / constant score).
  • \(0.50 < \text{AUC} < 0.70\): Weak separation (can still be valuable in noisy finance/ads!).
  • \(0.70 \le \text{AUC} < 0.80\): Acceptable / useful separation.
  • \(0.80 \le \text{AUC} < 0.90\): Strong separation.
  • \(\text{AUC} \ge 0.90\): Outstanding (if \(>0.99\), check for data leakage!).

3. Category 4 Zoom-In: 2×2 Confusion Table (100 Card Swipes)

Suppose out of 100 card swipes, 10 are truly stolen (\(y=1\)) and 90 are honest (\(y=0\)). At a fixed cutoff \(t\), our alarm flags 12 cards: 8 stolen (\(\text{TP}=8\)) and 4 honest mistakes (\(\text{FP}=4\)), leaving \(\text{FN}=2\) missed thefts and \(\text{TN}=86\) cleared cards:

Category 4 Metric Plain-English Question Fraction on Our 100 Cards
Accuracy Out of everyone, what fraction did we label right? \(\frac{\text{TP}+\text{TN}}{100} = \frac{8+86}{100} = 94\%\) (Trap: saying "0 for all" already gets \(90\%\)!)
Catch Rate / Recall / TPR Out of all real \(1\)s, what fraction did we catch? \(\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP}+\text{FN}} = \frac{8}{10} = 80\%\)
False Alarm Rate / FPR Out of all real \(0\)s, what fraction did we falsely flag? \(\frac{\text{FP}}{\text{FP}+\text{TN}} = \frac{4}{90} \approx 4.4\%\)
Precision Out of alarms that rang, what fraction were real \(1\)s? \(\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP}+\text{FP}} = \frac{8}{12} \approx 66.7\%\)

4. Practical Troubleshooting: Which Metric Solves Your Exact Goal?

Real-World Goal Category Metric to Pick Why Other Metrics Fail Here
Compare two models’ general ability to rank \(1\)s above \(0\)s before picking a threshold Cat 1 (Discrimination) ROC-AUC Hanley & McNeil, 1982 Accuracy depends on an arbitrary \(t=0.50\) cutoff and majority-class size
Catch rare \(0.1\%\) needles (fraud / rare disease) without flooding reviewers with false alarms Cat 1 (Rare-Event Rank) PR-AUC Saito & Rehmsmeier, 2015 ROC-AUC divides false alarms by huge \(\text{TN}\), making \(100\) false alarms look tiny
Price insurance, estimate clinical risk, or fit \(\hat{\beta}\) in a Logistic GLM Cat 2 + Cat 3 (Calibrated Probabilities) Log-Loss (\(= D/2n\)) + Calibration Plot Gneiting & Raftery, 2007 ROC-AUC and PR-AUC ignore monotonic rescaling (\(0.80\) vs. \(0.08\) gives identical AUC!)
Audit a deployed yes/no trigger rule where missing a case costs \(10\times\) a false alarm Cat 4 (Cutoff Action) Tune cutoff \(t\) for Cost / Net Benefit; report Recall & Precision @ \(t\) Default \(t=0.50\) falsely assumes false alarms and missed cases have equal \(1:1\) cost
