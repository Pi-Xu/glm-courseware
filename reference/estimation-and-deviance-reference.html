EDF Derivations, IRLS Estimation, Deviance & Quasi-Likelihood

Audited, self-contained mathematical and numerical reference for Exponential Dispersion Families, Cumulant Derivatives, Score/Fisher Scoring/IRLS, Deviance, and Overdispersion.

Grounded in Nelder & Wedderburn (1972), Wedderburn (1974) & Rodríguez App. B All numerical tables machine-verified

1. Exponential Dispersion Family (EDF) & Prior Weights \(a_i(\phi) = \phi / p_i\)

Nelder & Wedderburn’s Exponential Dispersion Family writes each observation’s density or PMF as Rodríguez §B.1.1, Eq. B.1:

\[ f(y_i \mid \theta_i, \phi) = \exp\!\left\{\frac{y_i \theta_i - b(\theta_i)}{a_i(\phi)} + c_i(y_i, \phi)\right\}, \qquad a_i(\phi) = \frac{\phi}{p_i}, \quad \phi > 0, \]

where \(p_i > 0\) is a known prior weight (usually \(p_i = 1\); for grouped proportions \(\tilde{y}_i = y_i/n_i\) or sample means, \(p_i = n_i\)). To rewrite any distribution into EDF form, apply \(\exp(\log f)\) and sort \(\log f\) into three strict buckets:

  1. Linear \(y_i\theta_i / a_i(\phi)\) bucket: Whatever parameter expression multiplies \(y_i / a_i(\phi)\) is the canonical parameter \(\theta_i\).
  2. Pure-\(\theta_i\) bucket \(-b(\theta_i)/a_i(\phi)\): Rewrite all remaining \(\theta_i\)-dependent terms purely as a function of \(\theta_i\) (watching the minus sign!).
  3. Remainder \(c_i(y_i, \phi)\): Collect all terms free of \(\theta_i\) (such as \(-\log(y_i!)\), \(-y_i^2/(2\sigma^2)\), or \(\log\binom{n_i}{y_i}\)).
Two Classic EDF Questions Answered
  • Why is Gamma’s natural parameter negative (\(\theta_i = -1/\mu_i < 0\)) instead of setting \(\phi < 0\)? In \(\log f = -\frac{\nu y_i}{\mu_i} - \nu\log\mu_i + \dots = \frac{y_i(-1/\mu_i) - \log\mu_i}{1/\nu} + c\), dispersion \(\phi = 1/\nu\) must be strictly positive because \(\operatorname{Var}(Y_i) = \frac{\phi}{p_i}b''(\theta_i) > 0\) and strict convexity forces \(b''(\theta_i) > 0\). Thus the minus sign must sit on \(\theta_i = -1/\mu_i < 0\), giving \(b(\theta_i) = \log(-1/\theta_i) = -\log(-\theta_i)\) (real-valued since \(-\theta_i > 0\)!).
  • Why do Rodríguez and McCullagh & Nelder write Binomial \(b(\theta)\) differently? For \(Y_i \sim \operatorname{Bin}(n_i, \pi_i)\) with \(\theta_i = \log\frac{\pi_i}{1-\pi_i}\): Form A (Counts \(y_i\), Rodríguez p. 8) sets \(p_i = 1\) and \(b_i(\theta_i) = n_i\log(1+e^{\theta_i})\) (\(\mu_i = n_i\pi_i\)). Form B (Proportions \(\tilde{y}_i = y_i/n_i\), McCullagh & Nelder Table 2.1) sets prior weight \(p_i = n_i\) and universal \(b(\theta_i) = \log(1+e^{\theta_i})\) (\(\mu_i = \pi_i, V(\pi_i) = \pi_i(1-\pi_i)\)). Both produce the exact same log-likelihood and IRLS weights \(w_i = n_i\pi_i(1-\pi_i)\); just never mix Form A’s \(b_i''\) with Form B’s \(p_i=n_i\)!

2. Cumulant Engine \(b(\theta)\), Bartlett’s Identities & Link Invertibility

  1. Role 1 (Log-Normalizer): Requiring \(\int f(y\mid\theta,\phi)\,dy = 1\) forces \(b(\theta) = a_i(\phi)\log\int \exp\{y\theta/a_i(\phi) + c_i(y,\phi)\}\,dy\) on the natural parameter space \(\Theta\) where the integral converges.
  2. Role 2 (Mean via Bartlett’s 1st Identity \(E[U(\theta)] = 0\)): Define the scalar \(\theta\)-score \(U(\theta) = \frac{\partial \ell_i}{\partial\theta_i} = \frac{Y_i - b'(\theta_i)}{a_i(\phi)}\). Differentiating \(\int f\,dy = 1\) under the fixed-support integral via \(\partial_\theta f = U\cdot f\) gives \(0 = E[U(\theta_i)] = \frac{E[Y_i] - b'(\theta_i)}{a_i(\phi)} \implies \mathbf{E[Y_i] = \mu_i = b'(\theta_i)}\).
  3. Role 3 (Variance via Bartlett’s 2nd Identity \(\operatorname{Var}(U) = -E[\partial_\theta^2 \ell]\)): Differentiating \(\int U f\,dy = 0\) a second time gives \(0 = \int [(\partial_\theta U)f + U(Uf)]\,dy = E[\partial_\theta^2 \ell_i] + E[U^2]\). Since \(E[U]=0\) and \(\partial_\theta^2 \ell_i = -\frac{b''(\theta_i)}{a_i(\phi)}\): \[ \frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)}{a_i(\phi)^2} = \operatorname{Var}(U) = -E\!\left[\frac{\partial^2 \ell_i}{\partial\theta_i^2}\right] = \frac{b''(\theta_i)}{a_i(\phi)} \implies \mathbf{\operatorname{Var}(Y_i) = \frac{\phi}{p_i}b''(\theta_i) = \frac{\phi}{p_i}V(\mu_i)}. \] Historical sidebar: The Cumulant Generating Function is \(K_Y(t) = \log E[e^{tY}] = \frac{b(\theta + t\,a_i(\phi)) - b(\theta)}{a_i(\phi)}\), so \(K_Y'(0) = b'(\theta)\) and \(K_Y''(0) = a_i(\phi)b''(\theta)\).
  4. Why \((b')^{-1}\) Always Exists (Strict Convexity): For any non-degenerate random variable, \(\operatorname{Var}(Y_i) > 0\) and \(\phi/p_i > 0\) imply \(b''(\theta) > 0\) everywhere on \(\Theta\). Thus \(b(\theta)\) is strictly convex, its derivative \(\mu = b'(\theta)\) is strictly increasing, and the Inverse Function Theorem guarantees a smooth one-to-one inverse \(\theta = (b')^{-1}(\mu)\). Choosing the canonical link \(g(\mu) = (b')^{-1}(\mu)\) composes \(\eta_i = g(b'(\theta_i)) = \theta_i\) directly!

3. Score Chain Rule, Observed vs. Expected Information & IRLS

Across \(n\) independent observations with joint log-likelihood \(\ell(\beta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ell_i(\theta_i(\beta))\), the 4-factor chain rule (\(\ell_i \to \theta_i \to \mu_i \to \eta_i \to \beta_j\)) gives the universal GLM score equations:

\[ \begin{gathered} U_j(\beta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \underbrace{\frac{\partial \ell_i}{\partial\theta_i}}_{\frac{p_i(y_i-\mu_i)}{\phi}} \underbrace{\frac{d\theta_i}{d\mu_i}}_{\frac{1}{V(\mu_i)}} \underbrace{\frac{d\mu_i}{d\eta_i}}_{\frac{1}{g'(\mu_i)}} \underbrace{\frac{\partial\eta_i}{\partial\beta_j}}_{x_{ij}} = \frac{1}{\phi}\sum_{i=1}^n w_i (y_i - \mu_i)\frac{d\eta_i}{d\mu_i}x_{ij}, \\[6pt] \text{or in matrix form:}\qquad U(\beta) = \frac{1}{\phi}X^\top W G (y - \mu) = 0, \end{gathered} \]

where \(G = \operatorname{diag}(d\eta_i/d\mu_i)\) and iterative weights are \(w_i = \frac{p_i}{V(\mu_i)(d\eta_i/d\mu_i)^2}\).

  • Canonical Link Collapse (\(\theta_i = \eta_i\)): Because \(\frac{d\mu_i}{d\eta_i} = V(\mu_i)\), \(V(\mu_i)\) cancels out of the score completely: \(U(\beta) = \frac{1}{\phi}X^\top P(y - \mu) = 0 \iff \mathbf{X^\top P \hat{\mu} = X^\top P y}\), making \(X^\top P y\) a \(p\)-dimensional minimal sufficient statistic (and forcing \(\sum(y_i - \hat{\mu}_i) = 0\) whenever \(X\) has an intercept and \(p_i = 1\)), and simplifying weights to \(w_i = p_i V(\mu_i)\).
  • Observed \(J(\beta)\) vs. Expected Fisher Information \(\mathcal{I}(\beta)\): Differentiating \(-U(\beta)\) gives: \[ J(\beta) = -\frac{\partial^2 \ell}{\partial\beta\partial\beta^\top} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{\phi}X^\top W X}_{\mathcal{I}(\beta) = E[J(\beta)]} - \frac{1}{\phi}\sum_{i=1}^n p_i(y_i - \mu_i)\frac{d^2\theta_i}{d\eta_i^2}x_i x_i^\top. \] Under any canonical link, \(\theta_i = \eta_i \implies \frac{d^2\theta_i}{d\eta_i^2} \equiv 0\), so Observed Information equals Expected Information identically (\(J(\beta) \equiv \mathcal{I}(\beta)\)) and Newton–Raphson \(\equiv\) Fisher Scoring! Under non-canonical links (e.g., Probit or Identity-Gamma), \(J(\beta) \neq \mathcal{I}(\beta)\) (and \(J\) can even fail to be positive definite away from \(\hat{\beta}\)), which is why GLM software uses Fisher Scoring (\(\mathcal{I}(\beta) = \frac{1}{\phi}X^\top W X\)).
  • Proof that Fisher Scoring \(\equiv\) IRLS (\(\phi\) cancels!): Writing \(\beta^{(t)} = (X^\top W^{(t)} X)^{-1}(X^\top W^{(t)} X)\beta^{(t)}\) (valid whenever \(\operatorname{rank}(X)=p\) and \(w_i > 0\)): \[ \beta^{(t+1)} = \beta^{(t)} + \mathcal{I}(\beta^{(t)})^{-1}U(\beta^{(t)}) = (X^\top W^{(t)} X)^{-1} X^\top W^{(t)} \underbrace{\big[X\beta^{(t)} + G^{(t)}(y - \mu^{(t)})\big]}_{z^{(t)}}. \] Row-by-row, \(z_i^{(t)} = \hat{\eta}_i^{(t)} + (y_i - \hat{\mu}_i^{(t)})\frac{d\eta_i}{d\mu_i}\) is the working response! Software initializes from data without guessing \(\beta^{(0)}\) via boundary-nudged \(\mu_i^{(0)}\) (e.g., \(y_i + 0.1\) for Poisson zeros; \(\frac{y_i+0.5}{n_i+1}\) for Binomial).
  • Resolving the Poisson Weight Paradox (\(w_i = \mu_i\)): Why do larger Poisson counts get larger IRLS weights \(w_i = \mu_i\) when \(\operatorname{Var}(Y_i) = \mu_i\) is noisier? Because IRLS works on the log scale \(z_i \approx \log y_i\), where relative variance is \(\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{Y_i - \mu_i}{\mu_i}\right) = \frac{\mu_i}{\mu_i^2} = \frac{1}{\mu_i}\)—large counts are much more precise in percentage/log terms!

4. Benchmark Models, Deviance, Residuals & Quasi-Likelihood

Comparing a fitted \(p\)-parameter model \(\omega\) (\(\hat{\mu}_i\)) against the \(n\)-parameter Saturated Model \(\Omega\) (\(\tilde{\mu}_i = y_i\), achieving maximum possible log-likelihood \(\ell(y;\phi)\)) cancels \(c_i(y_i,\phi)\) and defines Scaled Deviance \(D^*/\phi = -2[\ell(\hat{\mu};\phi) - \ell(y;\phi)] = D(y,\hat{\mu})/\phi\), where Unscaled Residual Deviance \(D(y,\hat{\mu})\) is completely free of \(\phi\):

\[ D(y, \hat{\mu}) = 2\sum_{i=1}^n p_i\big[y_i(\tilde{\theta}_i - \hat{\theta}_i) - b(\tilde{\theta}_i) + b(\hat{\theta}_i)\big] = \sum_{i=1}^n d_i \ge 0. \]
  • Gaussian: \(D = \sum (y_i - \hat{\mu}_i)^2 = \mathbf{RSS}\) (exact Residual Sum of Squares!).
  • Binomial: \(D = 2\sum\big[y_i\log\frac{y_i}{\hat{\mu}_i} + (n_i - y_i)\log\frac{n_i - y_i}{n_i - \hat{\mu}_i}\big]\) (with \(0\log 0 \equiv 0\)).
  • Poisson: \(D = 2\sum\big[y_i\log\frac{y_i}{\hat{\mu}_i} - (y_i - \hat{\mu}_i)\big]\).
  • Gamma: \(D = 2\sum p_i\big[-\log\frac{y_i}{\hat{\mu}_i} + \frac{y_i - \hat{\mu}_i}{\hat{\mu}_i}\big]\).

Three Inference & Dispersion Laws Never to Forget
  1. Ungrouped Binary (\(n_i=1\)) Deviance Trap: Comparing single-model \(D\) or \(X^2\) against \(\chi^2_{n-p}\) tests goodness-of-fit ONLY for grouped Binomial (\(n_i\) large) or moderate/large Poisson counts—never for ungrouped \(0/1\) binary data, where canonical-logit \(D = -2\sum[\hat{\pi}_i\log\hat{\pi}_i + (1-\hat{\pi}_i)\log(1-\hat{\pi}_i)]\) depends purely on \(\hat{\pi}_i\)! However, nested differences \(\Delta D = D(\omega_1) - D(\omega_2) \stackrel{a}{\sim} \chi^2_{p_2-p_1}\) remain valid even for ungrouped binary data.
  2. Geometric Trinity of Tests on \(\ell(\beta_1)\): Wald approximates curvature horizontally at \(\hat{\beta}_1\) (\(z = \hat{\beta}_1/\operatorname{SE}\), symmetric CI, fails under separation); LRT & Profile CI measure vertical deviance drop \(2[\ell(\hat{\beta}) - \ell_{\text{prof}}(\beta_1)] \le 3.84\) (asymmetric, parameterization-invariant); Score (Rao) checks slope \(U(\beta_0)\) at the null without fitting the larger model.
  3. \(\phi\)-Cancellation & Wedderburn’s (1974) Quasi-Likelihood: Because \(\phi\) cancels in \(U(\beta)=0\) and IRLS, \(\hat{\beta}\), \(\hat{\mu}\), and unscaled \(D\) do not depend on \(\phi\), whereas \(\operatorname{Cov}(\hat{\beta}) = \phi(X^\top\hat{W}X)^{-1}\) scales SEs by \(\sqrt{\phi}\)! Furthermore, since \(U(\beta)\) depends only on \(\mu_i\) and \(V(\mu_i)\), Quasi-Poisson (\(\operatorname{Var}(Y_i) = \phi\mu_i\) with \(\hat{\phi} = X^2/(n-p) > 1\)) gives consistent \(\hat{\beta}\) and valid SEs even though no discrete Poisson PMF exists for \(\phi \neq 1\)—at the cost of having no true likelihood or AIC.
